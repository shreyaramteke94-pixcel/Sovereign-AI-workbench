 POL-SEC-002
Security Incident Response Policy
Detection, reporting, containment, and escalation requirements for suspected security incidents.
Document Owner
IT Security and Risk Management
Classification
Internal — Enterprise Policy
Version
1.0
Effective Date
01 September 2026
Review Cycle
Annual or following a significant incident
Key Rule
When in doubt, report a suspected security incident to the IT security team promptly rather than attempting to
resolve or conceal it independently.
1. Purpose
 Establish a consistent process for identifying, reporting, containing, and responding to suspected security
incidents.
 Reduce the impact of incidents and preserve information needed for investigation.
2. What Constitutes a Security Incident
 Suspected unauthorized access to an organizational account or system.
 Loss or theft of an organization-owned device containing organizational information.
 Accidental disclosure or external sharing of Confidential or Restricted information.
 Suspected malware, ransomware, or other malicious software on an organization-managed device.
 Unexpected account activity, repeated login failures, or other signs of possible compromise.
3. Employee Reporting Requirements
 Employees must report suspected security incidents to the IT security team as soon as possible.
 Employees should provide the time of the event, affected device or account, observed symptoms, and any
relevant messages or evidence.
 Employees must not conceal or intentionally delay reporting a suspected incident.
4. Initial Response
 Do not delete potentially relevant files, logs, messages, or other evidence unless instructed by the IT security
team.
 Do not attempt unauthorized forensic investigation or system modification.
 If a device is suspected of being compromised, follow instructions from the IT security team regarding network
isolation or shutdown.
 If credentials may have been compromised, follow the organization’s account-security instructions
immediately.

5. Escalation
 The IT security team determines incident severity and coordinates technical response.
 High-impact incidents involving Confidential or Restricted information must be escalated to designated security
and management contacts.
 Incidents affecting critical business services may require additional continuity and recovery procedures.
6. Evidence and Confidentiality
 Incident information must be shared only with personnel who require it for response or management.
 Incident records, logs, and collected evidence must be stored in organization-approved systems.
 Employees must not publish incident details on social media or disclose them to external parties unless
authorized.
7. Recovery and Lessons Learned
 Systems may be returned to normal operation only after required security checks and authorization.
 Material incidents should be reviewed to identify corrective actions and improvements.
 Lessons learned may result in changes to controls, training, or organizational policies.
